MAKE IT A JOURNEY (the heart of his feedback):
1. The Flame travels. It is a small character on the path, standing on its stone with a soft contact shadow and the scale of the islands (not a glowing sticker floating over them). It moves from stone to stone with little game-like hops along the path (anticipation, arc, landing squash, a puff of dust or sparks), never teleporting.
2. Progress plays out: when the map opens and the Flame's level is higher than the last time the map was shown (store the last level shown in UserDefaults, key "map.lastLevel"; first time: no animation), the camera starts on the old stone and the Flame hops forward stone by stone to its new level while the camera follows smoothly; passed stones light up gold one by one as it lands on them; arriving at a new island's stone, that island wakes (a gentle glow, its badge appears).
3. Going somewhere is a journey too: choosing "Fly there" on an open island makes the Flame hop along the path to that island (quickly, the camera following; skip the walk if it's far: a quick dash with a trail), then the fly-in into the world as now (zoom toward the island, its light fills the screen, then the same model.go(...) call). Coming back to the map starts on the Flame.
4. Scrolling and every motion feel like a game: smooth momentum, no jumps, the camera eases when it moves on its own, a tap on the Flame makes it hop in place.

REDO THE BACKGROUND (he is not a fan of the current flat night-teal gradient with a turning swirl): design a better sky for this world, painted in the game's style and matching your sketches' mood but richer: for example a painted sea of clouds at dusk with depth layers (far mist, mid clouds, near wisps) moving at different speeds as you scroll (parallax), soft light from below, a few distant floating rocks. Paint the layers you need (magenta-keyed with prototypes/p2-spikes/tools/magenta_key.py, or opaque plates that tile vertically without visible seams), at the game's 82% saturation like scripts/p36-map-art.sh does, and keep image sizes reasonable.

60 FPS, EVERY ASPECT: the map must hold 60 fps on an iPhone 15 while scrolling, while the Flame hops, and during the fly-in. Avoid per-frame cost on many views (dozens of keyframeAnimators, .saturation/.colorMultiply/.brightness filters on big moving images, re-evaluating the whole map each frame); prefer pre-baked art, one TimelineView or Canvas driving the moving layers, drawingGroup where it helps, offscreen islands not animating. Run the app with `-perf` (it prints fps lines) in the simulator to compare variants; Claude will measure on the phone.
