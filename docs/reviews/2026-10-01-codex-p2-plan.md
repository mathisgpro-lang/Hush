# Independent P2 plan review — 2026-10-01

**Verdict: ready with fixes.** Resolve the BLOCKING findings before approving P2. The direction is feasible without new spending, but a pass under the current plan would not yet predict the production look or sustained performance.

Part 9 §3 is **partly met in all four sections**: §3.1 names the right features but misses some aliveness and representative-load checks; §3.2 covers most metrics but omits warm-up and memory-pressure procedures; §3.3 includes the asset trial, checklist, sound and art decision but omits the required schedule re-estimate; §3.4 does not give every spike an ordered fallback and retest gate. S3 correctly includes geometry, water, inhabitants, both inputs, all four combinations and Return.

Apple checks: the installed Xcode reports **27.0, build 27A266a**, matching B.1. Apple documents [RealityView, shader graphs and particles on iOS](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10103/) and [custom bloom through post-processing](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/288/). The installed SDK marks ShaderGraphMaterial as iOS 18+, CustomMaterial as iOS 15+, and PostProcessEffect as iOS 26+. These routes are available for iOS 26.4; this review did not run them on the phone or independently confirm its OS version. API availability does not establish the proposed combined effect's appearance or speed.

1. **BLOCKING — The placeholders can pass without proving the intended look (B.2–B.4, B.6; §3.1).** The hero has fluff across its body, luminous hair and detailed eyes; the Grove has dense foliage, layered mist, lanterns and reflective water. “40k triangles” does not define the cost of repeatedly drawing transparent layers over the same pixels. Nor is it clear whether the 15k character budget includes its 6–12 shells or whether the scene count includes the character. The final asset trial only explicitly returns to S1.

   **Fix:** define a representative combined scene at fixed portrait resolution and camera distances, including close-up and normal gameplay views. Specify total rendered geometry including shells, transparent coverage, material/texture counts, shadows, water treatment and peak ceremony particles. Run the trial Flame in S2/S3, with face, rig, hair, glow, mist and UI active together, on both rendering routes. Compare moving phone footage with the anchors and require Mathis's explicit visual acceptance. A smaller accepted scope is valid; it must be named, not reported as proof of every future stage or realm.

2. **BLOCKING — The measurement run cannot yet be repeated reliably (B.7; §3.2).** There is no warm-up, exact timing after the initial minute, repetition count, or defined Grove/ceremony workload. Memory-pressure testing is absent. The 2048² per-texture cap is not an aggregate texture-memory budget. “Frame time,” “hitch” and “total memory” lack measurement definitions; Allocations alone does not establish the whole app's footprint.

   **Fix:** prescribe a five-minute warm-up followed by an exact 30-minute timed script, fixed fake data/random seed, loading and unloading policy, and three runs per rendering route and full/reduced mode. Define presented-frame intervals, missed-frame/hitch thresholds, whole-process memory footprint and resident texture budget, with the instrument/export used for each. Specify background duration and a controlled device memory-pressure exercise with recovery criteria; distinguish a simulated warning from actual pressure. Save build/asset versions and raw traces. Separate uncharged, unplugged battery runs from profiling and screen recording; fix ambient temperature, starting charge range and cooling between runs. Report stalls and p99 limits as well as p95.

3. **BLOCKING — “Every aliveness criterion” is not covered (B.3, B.7; §3.1 and decision 93).** Time, season, mood and action awareness are not explicit test cases. Touch under 100 ms has no measurement method. Forcing quality modes does not by itself test automatic transitions, and “controls working” does not explicitly cover undo or truthful restoration snapshots.

   **Fix:** add controllable time/season/mood/action inputs and expected visible responses, including interrupted reactions and resume. Measure input-to-visible-response latency under load with timestamped instrumentation or sufficiently fast external video. Define every mode's effects and trigger precedence, then exercise the five-second step-down and 60-second recovery rules. In all five modes verify animation, completion, undo, navigation and a background image that updates when the world state changes.

4. **BLOCKING — Failure handling skips the agreed order and permits an unproven exit (B.3, B.9; §3.4, decision 97).** Rig blending jumps straight to step 3; S2/S3 have no explicit failure decisions. B.9 only requires a fallback to be chosen and shown, not to pass. Declining Blender leaves S1 partial without defining the consequence.

   **Fix:** give each spike an ordered failure table: simplify, painted/live hybrid where relevant, layered pre-rendered character, then a separately scoped Unity spike. Explain any inapplicable step. Require the selected substitute to repeat the relevant visual, aliveness and performance checks and receive Mathis's acceptance. A partial rig trial or failed fallback blocks the engine/art conclusion and P3 handoff.

5. **BLOCKING — The art exit lacks the required capacity check (A.2, B.6–B.9; §3.3).** “3–4 weeks” and roughly five short sessions are initial estimates, not a schedule revised from the trial. Source-file retention is also less explicit than export-script retention.

   **Fix:** retain the editable model, rig, animation, material and sound sources with tool versions. At exit, use measured work and revision time to re-estimate the P3 asset workload, founder approvals and phone sessions against Mathis's 1–2 hours/day. State the chosen no-spend compromises. Make that estimate an exit deliverable; seeking quotes must not silently authorize outreach or a paid trial.

6. **DEFERRABLE — Free signing works, but needs an operating checklist before installation (A.1, B.1).** One SpikeLab plus the real app fits Apple's limit. Apple currently documents **three apps/device, ten App IDs, three registered devices, and seven-day expiry**. [Apple account limits](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/basics/about-your-developer-account).

   **Fix:** call it one app throughout, inventory existing free-signed apps, keep one bundle ID/team and no extensions, and schedule weekly reprovisioning over the 3–4 weeks. Reinstall in place; never delete the real app to fix signing. Use bundled assets and existing AI allowances, pausing at quota limits rather than purchasing capacity.

7. **DEFERRABLE — A few implementation choices still need a bounded first experiment (B.2–B.6).** “Shader Graph or CustomMaterial,” unspecified Apple sample assets, and “usdchecker/realitytool” leave the actual material/export path open. Emissive material alone is not a complete bloom implementation.

   **Fix:** before producing the trial asset, pin the sample and its terms, Blender/export versions and validation commands; select the material route and an iOS-26-compatible bloom implementation. First prove one tiny skinned model with two blended clips, a changing face and displaced hair survives export and runs together. Keep the assembly steps reproducible, including shaders applied after import. Record unsupported effects and show their substitutes to Mathis; do not wait until the final art trial to discover an incompatible pipeline.
