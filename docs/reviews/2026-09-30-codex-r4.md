**B. Remaining issues — BLOCKING**

1. **16 §§B.8–B.9; 22 §B.4 — Cleanup can delete legitimate cloud data.**  
   **Problem:** A creates generation H’s zones before publishing H in Meta. B’s sweep sees current G and deletes H because H is “not current.” A can then publish H, leaving the current generation without its zones. A sweep using an earlier Meta snapshot can also delete H after it becomes current and receives data. “Not current” does not mean “safe to delete.”

   The protocol also contradicts itself: §B.8 says recreate on `zoneNotFound`; §B.9 forbids it. Generation creation publishes Meta after creating zones, whereas deletion publishes Meta before creating them. The concurrent-deletion loser can clear its intent merely because retirement is recorded, before cloud erasure is confirmed.

   **Concrete fix:** Define one generation lifecycle in Meta: proposed, active, irrevocably retired/abandoned. Sweep only generations irrevocably marked for deletion, never merely noncurrent ones. Make publication and recovery ordering consistent; prohibit ordinary recreation everywhere. Bind deletion intents to the account and persist cleanup obligations until zone deletion is confirmed. Add these exact interleavings to the required tests.

2. **16 §§B.5–B.6; 17 §§B.3–B.4 — HealthKit identifiers still cross the privacy boundary.**  
   **Problem:** P envelopes explicitly forbid HealthKit sample references, yet auto-detected completions sync `occurrenceKey = hk:{sampleUUID}`. Moving `Evidence` into H2 did not remove this second reference.

   **Concrete fix:** Keep the sample UUID and its mapping in H2. Specify an app-owned occurrence identity and how cross-device auto-detection avoids duplicate credit. Any proposed HealthKit-derived synced token needs an explicit classification decision; hashing alone does not make the existing rule consistent.

   **Platform uncertainty:** Decision 59 authorizes the product’s intended disclosure; it does not establish Apple’s permission. The written-answer gate must cover activity facts, catalog references, derived difficulty and deduplication identifiers. Apple’s prohibition on personal health information in iCloud remains explicit. [App Review §5.1.3(ii)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

3. **04 §§B.1–B.4; 17 §B.4 — Reconciliation can retract earned growth without a correction.**  
   **Problem:** Device A deals one slot and completes it: **G = 1.01**. An earlier-recorded plan from B arrives with a different slot. B wins; A’s completion becomes fill: **G = 1.005**. I reproduced both values with the reference calculator. A replacement Spark can cause the same demotion. Competing plans can also change the day’s `rulesVersion`.

   Earliest-completion deduplication can similarly replace a higher-difficulty completion with a lower-difficulty duplicate. The Depth monotonicity proof applies after preprocessing; it does not protect against this replacement.

   **Concrete fix:** Preserve the scoring contracts under which work was completed. Define correction-aware reconciliation that cannot reduce earned credit—for example, evaluate issued day contracts separately and retain the capped maximum earned completion. Preserve contribution values against duplicate replacement. If reconciliation is instead allowed to reduce earned scores, Mathis must explicitly approve that exception.

4. **04 §B.1; 17 §B.8; 16 §B.5 — Days without app execution lack durable scoring inputs.**  
   **Problem:** `dayPlanned` is created at background execution or first opening that day. Neither is guaranteed. A planned rest day can therefore leave no P record, and later appears as absence. After phone loss, the H2 plan cannot recover it. Background scheduling does not close this gap: Apple explicitly does not guarantee execution at the requested time. [BGTaskRequest documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgtaskrequest/earliestbegindate?changes=_3)

   **Concrete fix:** Persist sufficient P schedule structure when the plan is accepted, including effective dates, rest status and scoring/version inputs. Materialize missed days deterministically without requiring an app launch or surviving H2 prose. Keep an explicit distinction between genuinely unplanned days and planned days lacking execution.

5. **04 §B.5 — Completing the fourth comeback day dims the Flame.**  
   **Problem:** After 14 absent days and three full comeback days, the reference produces:

   | Moment | Vitality |
   |---|---:|
   | Day 3 completed | 1.000000 |
   | Day 4 before completing anything | 1.000000 |
   | Day 4 completed | **0.732292** |

   Completing day 4 changes `n` from 3 to 4, removing comeback protection. This contradicts the approved return-to-full behavior and makes the reward moment punish completion.

   **Concrete fix:** Carry the relight state through the transition into ordinary Vitality. A positive completion must not remove protection and lower the displayed value. Define when protection subsequently decays after actual absence; test days 1–14, including morning versus post-completion values and planned rest.

6. **04 §A.8 and §§B.4–B.5 — Planned rest’s +1% remains an unapproved founder decision.**  
   **Problem:** The chapter explicitly labels this “proposed,” but Part B already assigns rest `completion = 1`, which awards +1%. Decision 52 approves protection from dimming; it does not expressly approve growth credit for rest.

   **Concrete fix:** Obtain and log Mathis’s decision. Represent growth completion and Vitality presence separately if rest protects Vitality without earning +1%. Do not treat the current formula as approval.

**B. Remaining issues — DEFER-TO-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN**

1. **04 §B.6; 17 §B.3 — Finish level replay and normalize weekly difficulty.**  
   A weekly level of 30 across three sessions gives a session target of 10, then the written formula produces `d = 10/30 = 0.333333`, rather than 1.0. Divide by the corresponding per-session baseline. Define durable track association for generated slots, duplicate-feedback resolution, initialization and same-day counter handling. Test third-completion plus easy/hard feedback and corrections. This repairs the stated intent without requiring a new product choice.

2. **16 §B.5; 04 §B.6 — Correct the level-recovery claim.**  
   Replaying identical percentage changes from different replacement seeds does not generally make levels converge. Specify fresh calibration when H2 seeds are lost and remove the convergence claim. Test that recalibration affects future targets while preserved `d` values keep historical scores unchanged. Chapter 07’s safety gate remains mandatory.

3. **04 §B.8; reference script; 17 §B.7 — Turn arithmetic examples into engine fixtures.**  
   The script takes already-processed actions/completions. Its correction examples simply pass smaller totals; they do not exercise corrections. Add complete entries, versions, `asOf`, settings, graph processing and permutations. Use full-precision expected values and one explicit tolerance: current checks use `1e-6` or `1e-5`, despite the `1e-9` contract. Also remove “the result is tie-independent”: unequal recency weights make the specified tie-break consequential.

4. **04 §B.5; 16 §B.3 — Bound sensory-event deduplication honestly.**  
   Synced `momentWitnessed` entries cannot prevent two disconnected devices from both presenting the same moment before exchanging acknowledgements. Specify once per installation plus suppression after acknowledgement arrives, or require coordination. Add the offline-device case; do not promise global exactly-once presentation from an eventual-sync marker.

**Platform checks:** Apple’s documentation supports the PCC offering, eligibility requirements, entitlement and TestFlight availability; that architectural premise is supported. [Apple PCC eligibility](https://developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute/) Actual entitlement/device availability still needs validation. I have not established a CloudKit atomic operation combining Meta publication with zone creation/deletion; real-device testing remains necessary and cannot substitute for fixing the sweep’s logical race.
