# Independent review of Plan 2.0

Reviewed 2026-10-01. Scope: overview and Parts 1–8, decisions 84–92, and the growth model in chapter 04 Part A. I also checked selected inherited architecture, data and safety rules where the plan relies on them, and current Apple and Unity documentation. This is a planning review, not a code audit or a measured device-performance result.

The direction is coherent and worth pursuing. Keeping the record and growth engine separate from the world is the right foundation. The plan is **not yet a reliable building contract**: phase boundaries disagree, discovery is promised but barely specified, and several story rules change the mechanics they claim only to illustrate. P2 can proceed after its spike criteria and scope are made explicit; the later blockers do not require finishing the whole universe before experimenting.

Here, **BLOCKING** means resolve before building or releasing the affected feature, with the gate named in each finding. **DEFERRABLE** means it can wait for the named phase plan. Recommendations below are proposals, not new founder decisions.

| Part | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — Overview | ready with fixes | Captures the direction, but retains superseded recommendations and overstates what stays unchanged. |
| 1 — Integrated experience | not ready | Discovery, progress timing, permanent restoration and access to core functions need explicit rules. |
| 2 — World bible | ready with fixes | Strong setting; Beacon, Act and absence rules conflict with mechanics and ethics. |
| 3 — Character bible | ready with fixes | Useful character direction; production states, permanent stages and pressure-bearing copy need tightening. |
| 4 — Art, sound and feel | ready with fixes | Sufficient for concepts, conditional on an achievable rendering target and accessible functional layouts. |
| 5 — Technical architecture | not ready | Spike acceptance, restoration inputs, fallback integration and delivery contracts are incomplete. |
| 6 — Production | ready with fixes | Honest about specialist craft and costs; needs a concrete no-spend path and production acceptance checklist. |
| 7 — Roadmap | not ready | P3 cannot currently deliver its stated Act I finish line; P4/P5 evidence and safety gates disagree. |
| 8 — Master-plan rewrites | not ready | Contains direct conflicts with decisions 88 and 91 and insufficient mappings for the new mechanics. |

1. **BLOCKING — One phase contract must replace the conflicting scope lists.**

   **Where:** Part 8 §2, “23 MVP”; Part 7 P3–P5; Part 1 §5; overview §4; decisions 88 and 91.

   **Why it matters:** Part 8 puts TestFlight, iCloud and the Relay in P4 “for the circle.” Decision 88 and Part 7 keep P4 on Mathis’s phone, offline/local. Part 1 still calls all eight Journeys the MVP library, including running and strength, while Part 7 contains only three. The overview preserves the old recommendation to pay Apple at P4 beside the newer answer. An agent could build the wrong phase while following a plausible source.

   **Concrete fix:** Before the next phase plan, publish one capability-by-phase table covering Journeys, realms, counsel, sharing, storage, downloads and audience. Make P3/P4 bundled, curated and local; move paid-program services to P5. Label the eight-Journey table as a future catalog, and label old recommendations as superseded. Link every scope list to that table.

2. **BLOCKING — P3 promises all of Act I but omits its ending and locations.**

   **Where:** Part 2 §§5.2, 5.5, 7; Part 7 P3/P4; Part 8 §2.

   **Why it matters:** Act I ends at the Forge and the Age of Metal. P3 promises that Mathis lives Act I, but the Forge ceremony is in P4. The Book lives in the Library and the Forge ceremony at Forge Mountain; neither realm is in P3, and neither is guaranteed in P4. A six-week test can reach a scene that does not exist.

   **Concrete fix:** Before P3, choose whether it ends before the Forge or includes a small Sanctuary-hosted Forge ceremony. Keep the Book accessible from the Sanctuary independently of the Library’s release or unlock. List every Act I beat, its exact engine trigger, scene, required assets and replay location. Do not require building two extra full realms just to finish the slice.

3. **BLOCKING — The one-Journey slice cannot validate discovery.**

   **Where:** Part 3 §11, first question; Part 7 P3; Part 1 §§5, 7; decision 92.

   **Why it matters:** Asking what would make someone’s year feel alive and then offering only a relationship Journey is effectively a predetermined goal. Decision 92 explicitly requires a curated library broad enough to match answers and learning from behavior, without AI. No rule explains how text becomes a choice, how unsupported goals are handled, or how skips change recommendations. The inherited three-card fallback is not a complete discovery design.

   **Concrete fix:** Before P3, specify a short, curated selection flow with several safe interests, an “I’m not sure” route and an honest unsupported-goal route. Let the user confirm the suggested Journey. Keep only the Grove at final scene quality if necessary, but allow other safe goals to work through the Sanctuary. Define what is remembered, how suggestions change, and how users correct them. A skip must not automatically mean dislike or permission to choose a different life goal.

4. **BLOCKING — Beacons have several incompatible unlock rules, and force balance.**

   **Where:** Part 1 §5 finales and §8; Part 2 §§2, 6–7; Part 3 §3; Part 8 glossary; chapter 04 §A.7.

   **Why it matters:** Beacons are variously earned by finishing great Journeys, fully restoring a realm, or an ability unlocked at Kindled. A Heart Journey’s finale already relights the great lantern that is the Grove’s Beacon. Meanwhile all six Beacons gate Act III. That makes developing every pillar compulsory for major story access, contradicting “balance is shown, never forced.” It also risks asking people to pursue physical goals that do not suit them.

   **Concrete fix:** Before Journey progression is built, give each Beacon one exact condition and distinguish Journey landmarks from realm Beacons. State whether Kindled is required or merely an animation form. Recommend allowing the main story to advance through personally chosen pillars, with all six Beacons an optional completion goal. This changes an approved default and needs an explicit decision, not a silent engineering interpretation.

5. **BLOCKING — The restoration shader has only one input for two different meanings.**

   **Where:** Part 1 §§2–3, 8; Part 4 §3; Part 5 §§B.2–B.3, B.7.

   **Why it matters:** Depth restores the world permanently; Vitality changes its recent atmosphere. Yet the proposed `hush` parameter is driven only by restoration and controls saturation, fog and emissives. It cannot independently show a highly restored realm during an absence, or an unrestored realm while Vitality is high. The Return also clears the Sanctuary mist immediately, whereas the growth engine returns to full Vitality over about three days.

   **Concrete fix:** Before S3, specify separate restoration and Vitality inputs, plus a temporary Return effect. Test all combinations of low/high restoration and low/high Vitality. Permanent landmarks stay present; the first return can briefly clear foreground mist without pretending the underlying Vitality value is already full.

6. **BLOCKING — Corrections and content updates can break “permanent” world progress.**

   **Where:** Part 1 §§8, 10; Part 3 §3; Part 5 §B.2; chapter 04 §§A.5, A.7; inherited chapter 16 §B.3.

   **Why it matters:** Chapter 04 correctly allows corrections to reduce calculated growth. Plan 2.0 calls restoration a direct view of Depth but says it never decreases; it also promises stages and keepsakes are never revoked. Recomputing everything from corrected Depth cannot satisfy both statements by itself. The foundation already distinguishes permanent grants from current scores, but the new world does not map its landmarks and levels onto that distinction. Changing a catalog threshold creates the same problem.

   **Concrete fix:** Before P3, specify which things are permanent grants and which are current calculated values. Reuse existing grant records for owned stages, Ages, landmarks and any permanently retained restoration milestones; define the visual floor after a correction. Version Journey definitions and threshold rules so catalog updates do not silently move old roads or remove earned content. Add examples for undo just after a threshold, a later correction and a changed catalog.

7. **BLOCKING — Progress presentation is not yet an exact mapping of the engine.**

   **Where:** Part 1 §§3, 5–6, 8; Part 5 §B.2; chapter 04 §§A.3–A.5.

   **Why it matters:** Height cannot tick upward on every completion after the daily 1% ceiling. Rest days earn growth without a completed Action. One Action may feed multiple pillars, but the animation sends light to one realm. The map changes immediately in one paragraph and during the evening ritual in another. “Steps completed / plan structure” does not define progress through rescheduled, shortened, repeated or abandoned plans. At restoration level 5, the promise that every Action visibly restores more also has no defined behavior.

   **Concrete fix:** Before P3, write worked examples for those cases. Save completion and update truth immediately; evening only summarizes. Show Height changing only when it really changes, and use a separate acknowledgement when capped. Define multi-pillar routing, rest presentation, stable Journey waypoint IDs and plan-revision behavior. At maximum restoration, celebrate contribution without inventing a new score increase.

8. **BLOCKING — Passing the proposed spikes would not prove the production look.**

   **Where:** Part 3 §§2, 6; Part 4 §4; Part 5 §§A.4, B.3, B.7.

   **Why it matters:** A blob rig and simple water plane can pass while fluffy translucent fire, facial expressions, overlapping particles, soft shadows, glow and restoration changes remain unproven. Avoiding blend shapes is a sensible simplification, but the actual bone/texture face still needs testing during body animation. A material parameter can tint an existing object; it does not itself restore geometry, add creatures or make a waterfall start flowing. Apple documents custom shader hooks, not a guarantee that this complete art direction is cheap. [Apple custom-material documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit/modifying-realitykit-rendering-using-custom-materials)

   **Concrete fix:** Before approving P2, add a feature checklist tied to the exact installed Xcode and iOS version. S1 must combine representative fur/fire coverage, facial changes, rig blending and embers. S2 must use the expected scene density with SwiftUI controls. S3 must restore a visible object, flowing water and inhabitants as well as tint, using both inputs from finding 5. Free placeholders are fine if they reproduce the expensive properties. Record unsupported effects and approved substitutes; do not count a simpler look as a pass without Mathis seeing the difference.

9. **BLOCKING — The performance budget is not reproducible and omits peak use.**

   **Where:** Part 5 §§A.4, B.3; Part 7 P3 finish line.

   **Why it matters:** S2 says “<150 MB memory”; B.3 says “≤150 MB per scene.” Neither defines total app memory or the peak while replacing one scene with another. Triangle and light counts miss texture memory, many transparent layers, material count and shader cost. A momentary 60 fps reading does not prove sustained operation after the phone warms up. The battery target lacks brightness, device condition and workload settings.

   **Concrete fix:** In the P2 plan, define total-process steady and peak memory, texture budgets and scene-transition behavior. Record frame times and visible stalls through a repeated 30-minute scenario with loading, completion and ceremonies, plus background/foreground and memory-pressure tests. Fix brightness, sound, power mode and test conditions for battery comparisons. Treat the current numbers as proposed targets to validate, not established limits. Require a measured full-quality result and a measured reduced-quality result.

10. **BLOCKING — Unity is an untested second architecture, not a ready fallback.**

   **Where:** Overview §3g; Part 5 §§A.2–A.3; Part 7 risk table; decision 84.

   **Why it matters:** Unity documents full-screen-only rendering for this integration, retained memory of roughly 80–180 MB after unloading, and inability to restart its runtime after quitting within the same iOS app session. That affects the native UI composition, memory budget and lifecycle. The pure growth rules can stay, but a safe Swift-to-Unity communication boundary still has to be designed. [Unity integration limitations](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/platform-specific/cross-platform-features/unityasa-library)

   **Concrete fix:** Before selecting Unity, require its own bounded spike: world plus native controls, one completion round trip, navigation, background/resume, unloading, haptics and accessibility. Specify that only Swift writes the record. If RealityKit misses a particular effect, first price a simpler rendering treatment; do not switch the entire engine automatically. Also assess pre-rendered 3D animation as a proposal: decision 82 explicitly allowed it, although Part 5 dismisses the 2.5D option as incapable of the desired look. Changing decision 84’s fallback requires Mathis’s agreement.

11. **BLOCKING — The low-performance fallback is three different products.**

   **Where:** Part 5 §§B.3, B.6; Part 8 §4; Part 4 §2.

   **Why it matters:** One passage retains a 3D character over a still, another keeps the old 2D Flame for low-power mode, and “alive, always” implies continuous motion. If the character shader is the bottleneck, freezing the background may not help. A still must also represent the user’s restored places and keepsakes accurately.

   **Concrete fix:** Before P3, define ordered quality modes, their triggers and their asset requirements: full scene; reduced effects; static scene with a cheaper character; fully accessible static presentation if needed. Make thermal pressure, Low Power Mode and Reduce Motion distinct inputs. Define how the fallback represents progression and how it returns to higher quality without repeated switching. All modes must support completion, undo and navigation.

12. **BLOCKING — Safe-first scope and qualified-review timing are unresolved.**

   **Where:** Part 7 P2/P4; Part 8 §2, Companion; Part 1 §5; decision 91; chapter 07 safety rules.

   **Why it matters:** Part 8 blocks every health Journey until qualified review; decision 91 permits gentle sourced habits now and places training review before launch. Part 7 P4 instead depends on a reviewed running Journey. Calling Heart, Spirit or World content “safe” also does not remove the need for safe alternatives: relationship prompts can touch unsafe relationships, and calm practices or outdoor tasks do not suit everyone. The unchanged difficulty engine gradually increases targets; a public guideline alone is not a personalized safety limit.

   **Concrete fix:** Before any new Journey is made usable, define an allowed-content table: gentle curated habits now, training content disabled until its gate, and restricted advice never improvised. For each shipped template specify limits, adaptation permissions, stop/skip alternatives and relevant referral wording. Apply these checks to Encounters and Rituals too, including combined demands across Paths. Reconcile the review gate with decision 91 explicitly. Finding a reviewer is not the same as receiving approval of the actual content version.

13. **BLOCKING — The Hush and cast can induce guilt without saying “you failed.”**

   **Where:** Part 2 §§1–2, 4, 5.3, 5.5; Part 1 Encounters; Part 3 §§1, 11.

   **Why it matters:** The Flame is “the part of you that’s alive,” while the Hush settles where people do not “live fully.” That equates unlogged time with reduced aliveness. “I’ve never seen one come back. Show me,” the lonely fox, and “Talent’s cheap. Show me the hours” create obligation or judgment. Illness, caring responsibilities and simply living without tracking can then feel like neglecting oneself or a dependent creature.

   **Concrete fix:** Before final script and sound production, rewrite those examples around recorded activity rather than personal worth. A creature’s wellbeing must never depend on completing its request. Give every request a neutral decline and ensure return dialogue is welcoming without debt. Preserve the approved soul metaphor, but make clear that life outside the app counts even when unrecorded. Review the absence scene with people who take long breaks, not only daily enthusiasts.

14. **BLOCKING — Calendar rewards have no protection against missing-out pressure.**

   **Where:** Part 1 §§4, 9; Part 2 §§7–8; Part 3 §9; Part 5 `Keepsake.window`.

   **Why it matters:** Seasonal keepsakes, new-moon Encounters and dated Gatherings imply deadlines without explaining replay, catch-up or availability. The absence of streak counters does not prevent fear of missing out. Part 1 also says the story advances an Act each year “for everyone,” contradicting Part 2’s lived-time, never-calendar rule.

   **Concrete fix:** Before shipping timed content, separate calendar atmosphere from personal progression. Remove automatic annual Act advancement. Specify availability and replay: recommend that stories and equivalent rewards remain earnable later, with dates recording when earned rather than creating permanent exclusion. Give Encounters a persistent or repeatable route and avoid countdowns or guilt notifications. Decide pause behavior at seasonal boundaries and during long absences.

15. **BLOCKING — Essential controls cannot depend on scenic navigation.**

   **Where:** Part 1 §7; Part 3 §6; Part 4 §5; Part 7 P5 accessibility pass.

   **Why it matters:** “No tabs, no menus” leaves no discoverable route to Settings, backup, free Actions, changing a Journey or late corrections. A home ember only solves getting home. Spatial scrolls and glyph transitions may fail with large text or VoiceOver. Waiting until P5 for accessibility can require redesigning the central interaction.

   **Concrete fix:** Before P3 layouts, define labeled routes to each essential task, with a plain SwiftUI list alternative. Ensure quick tap works alongside hold, glyphs remain decorative to VoiceOver, and text is immediately readable in the accessible mode. Test completion, correction, backup and return navigation with VoiceOver, large text and Reduce Motion during P3. Keep world travel as presentation around these controls.

16. **BLOCKING — P5 is a major integration phase with incompatible success evidence.**

   **Where:** Part 7 P4/P5; Part 8 §2; inherited chapter 23 §A.5; decision 88.

   **Why it matters:** Friends briefly trying Mathis’s phone cannot establish four-week retention or recovery across devices. A 2–3-week TestFlight round cannot measure the retained week-four criterion. P5 introduces sync, live AI, downloads and the Relay together; these are privacy and data-integrity boundaries, not merely switches. Deferring the Apple fee is feasible, but it leaves real integration risk for P5.

   **Concrete fix:** Before approving the roadmap, split founder evidence from external-beta evidence. Keep the agreed fee timing. Use local fakes and failure tests earlier, then require real-service gates in P5 for offline conflicts, deletion, restore, AI unavailability and download failures. Make the short TestFlight round a minimum that extends when a gate fails. Either measure week-four retention in a longer round or explicitly replace that metric; do not declare it passed from same-phone demonstrations.

17. **BLOCKING — Paying Apple does not by itself establish the launch AI path.**

   **Where:** Overview §4; Part 6 §5; Part 7 P5; Part 8 Companion rewrite; decision 92’s launch promise.

   **Why it matters:** Current Apple documentation does provide a PCC API; it is not correct to reject this plan on the old assumption that none exists. However, Apple requires Small Business Program enrollment, download eligibility and an assigned PCC entitlement, and specifies supported distribution routes. The older architecture mentions eligibility; Plan 2.0’s “join, then turn on” wording hides it. Actual OS/device/region availability on Mathis’s iPhone remains a test obligation. [Apple PCC eligibility](https://developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute/)

   **Concrete fix:** Before promising launch behavior, carry the eligibility checks and curated fallback into the phase contract. At P5 test a real request through an approved distribution route on the target phone, plus unavailable, offline and quota-exhausted cases. Do not promise that any-goal plan generation necessarily becomes available just because membership is paid. Provider changes still need the existing privacy and safety review.

18. **DEFERRABLE — Asset delivery needs one chosen route and a failure contract.**

   **Where:** Part 5 §B.4; Part 8 architecture/security rows; Part 7 P4/P5.

   **Why it matters:** “Only Sanctuary ships” conflicts with decision 88 until P5. “On-Demand Resources / Background Assets” leaves two different systems unresolved. Apple now documents On-Demand Resources as deprecated from iOS 27 and describes Apple-hosted Background Assets as the replacement direction. [Apple resource status](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-uploads/on-demand-resources-size-limits/), [Background Assets](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/BackgroundAssets)

   **Concrete fix:** Correct the prelaunch scope now, then settle delivery in the P5 plan. Prefer evaluating Background Assets rather than starting a new ODR implementation. Define package versions, storage limits, retry, eviction, unavailable-network screens and compatibility with the installed app. Keep the signed catalog rules already specified in chapter 16; distinguish that Relay-hosted catalog from Apple-hosted art. Users must still log progress when a realm package is absent.

19. **BLOCKING — No-spend concepts do not yet form a production pipeline.**

   **Where:** Parts 3 §10 and §11 production notes; Part 4 §8; Part 6 §§2–4; Part 7 P2/P3; decision 87.

   **Why it matters:** AI-only through P2 is consistent with decision 87; human art later is not inherently a contradiction. The missing branch is what happens if Mathis still chooses no spend at P2’s exit. “Final quality” requires rigged forms, facial states, music and sound, not just attractive images. Free assets also need usable licenses and compatible exports. An engine pass cannot prove that a solo founder and AI agents can deliver the approved art.

   **Concrete fix:** Before P3 approval, require one representative end-to-end asset trial: concept to rig/animation/material to phone, with source files, license, reproducible export and measured cost in time. Include a playable sound sample. Offer a bounded no-spend continuation with explicit quality compromises if the budget remains zero. Keep freelance routes conditional; no automatic outreach or paid trial. Create an asset acceptance checklist for dimensions, rig names, animations, textures, performance and rights. Re-estimate the schedule from this trial and Mathis’s 1–2 hours/day.

20. **BLOCKING — “Protected by backup” needs a demonstrated recovery route.**

   **Where:** Part 7 P4; Part 1 §10; decision 88; Part 5 migration.

   **Why it matters:** The decision authorizes local-only use and weekly re-signing, but it does not establish that backups are enabled, current or sufficient for every new world field. An export alone is not recovery until import works. Replacing the app, changing signing identity or testing migration must not be improvised against Mathis’s only record.

   **Concrete fix:** Before P3 stores real weeks of use, document the stable installation/re-sign procedure and verify export/import in a throwaway installation. Check growth, grants, name, settings, Journey revisions and witnessed scenes. Explain what an ordinary device backup includes under the existing privacy exclusions and what may be lost. Keep a restorable copy before migrations; never solve signing trouble by deleting the real app without a verified recovery plan.

21. **DEFERRABLE — Local Gatherings and later sharing need honest presentation.**

   **Where:** Part 1 §§2, 4, 8–9; Part 2 Grove and world rule 8; Part 7 P3/P4.

   **Why it matters:** “Everyone’s lights” suggests actual participants, but the agreed early Gathering is local. Decorative lanterns could imply fabricated social proof. Later, another person’s world would reveal self-reported pillar progress, conflicting with the existing rule that shareable/status-bearing achievements must be verified.

   **Concrete fix:** Specify before P3 that local lights represent the user or are clearly scenery, with no implied live count. Before social development, define separate private and shareable views, consent and the verified fields allowed outside the device. Do not expose the full private world merely because it already renders locally.

22. **DEFERRABLE — Character variants and ceremonies need a bounded specification.**

   **Where:** Part 3 §§3–6, 9, 11; Part 5 §§B.2, B.5.

   **Why it matters:** The Spark has no arms yet must catch and throw light. “Chooses its name” conflicts mildly with the user naming it. Top-two-pillar blending has no tie or switching rule, and costume fit across forms can multiply production work. “Every animation interruptible” does not explain whether a skipped or interrupted ceremony counts as witnessed. Multiple milestones may become eligible together after import or a long absence.

   **Concrete fix:** Resolve in the P3 character/state plan: animation variants per stage, a bounded set of shapes, stable tie handling, accessory attachment points and optional naming behavior. Specify ceremony priority, skip, crash/resume and replay, keeping reward ownership separate from seeing its animation. Later-stage fine detail can wait, but the shared rig and event contract cannot.

23. **DEFERRABLE — Pacing claims need simulated lives, not calendar labels.**

   **Where:** Overview §1; Part 1 §8; Part 2 §7; Part 3 §3; Part 7 P3–P6.

   **Why it matters:** Restoration at 400 Depth in each of six pillars, Kindled at 300 active days plus Depth 800, and Acts described as “months” or “year 2” have not been reconciled with the allowed daily workload. P3 only produces Grove levels 0–3, yet six weeks of some legitimate schedules could reach farther. Fixed story dates would either rush the engine or leave people staring at unfinished content.

   **Concrete fix:** During P2/P3 planning, run the existing engine against specialist, balanced, low-frequency, planned-rest, pause and comeback histories. Report when each threshold is reached and what content exists then. Label calendar estimates as illustrative. Define an honest upper-content boundary while later levels are being produced; do not silently cap real growth or hide earned rewards to fit the schedule.

24. **DEFERRABLE — The growth number needs a narrower claim, and success needs more than retention.**

   **Where:** Part 1 §2, “one honest number”; §9 retention table; chapter 04 §§A.1, A.5; Part 7 finish lines.

   **Why it matters:** ×1.37 is a defined app score, not evidence that someone became 37% healthier, wiser or more valuable. The soul metaphor makes that distinction especially important. Daily return and affection for the character alone could reward pressure rather than usefulness.

   **Concrete fix:** Before public copy and evaluation, explain Height as a record-based game measure, without changing the approved formula. Add voluntary feedback on usefulness outside the app, freedom to skip, ease of returning and whether challenges fit the person. Treat “I felt obliged to keep the Flame alive” as a problem even if retention is high. This does not require analytics or collecting personal records.

The next useful deliverable is a corrected P2 phase plan with representative spike fixtures, measurable acceptance criteria and an explicit art-production decision at its exit. P3 should remain gated on the discovery flow, Act I scope, restoration contract and safe content rules above.
