**B. Findings**

The first five include newly exposed failure cases in still-open protocols; they are identified as continuations rather than counted as wholly new defects.

**1. Critical — The health classification still cannot be implemented consistently.**  
**Files:** 16 §B.5; 17 §§B.2–B.5, B.8. **Continuation: R1 #1.**

**Problem:** `actionCompleted` is always P but embeds `Evidence`, including HealthKit provenance and sample references. `pathStarted/pathRevised` embed `goal`, and `sparkDealt` embeds prompt text, although personal free text must be H2. Non-Body `PlanRevision`s remain categorically P despite potentially containing personal generated text. Every row has one `healthClass`, so “free text is always H2” does not explain how these mixed rows are stored.

Additionally, 16 calls a difficulty scalar derived from an H2 level an “accepted” P disclosure. Decision 57 does not explicitly authorize that exception to its health-derived-calibration boundary.

**Why it matters:** Different builders could legitimately implement opposite storage policies, including uploading data the founder designated phone-only.

**Concrete fix:** Define separate P envelopes and H2 attachments, with exact fields in each. Keep HealthKit evidence out of P completion payloads. Explicitly settle the minimum synced difficulty representation against decision 57; do not describe an undocumented exception as accepted.

**Platform uncertainty:** Decision 57 accepts the risk around syncing activity facts. That is not proof Apple permits them—or derived difficulty scalars. Apple still prohibits personal health information in iCloud; the written-answer gate remains necessary. [App Review §5.1.3(ii)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**2. Critical — Restoring P entries is insufficient to restore the growth number.**  
**Files:** 04 §§B.3–B.4; 16 §B.5; 17 §§B.3, B.8. **Continuation: R1 #2.**

**Problem:** G needs the entire scheduled set `S(D)`, including **uncompleted** items and their difficulties. Vitality needs historical planned-rest status. Those facts may exist only in an H2 Body plan.

Example: a day has two scheduled Body items, each difficulty 1; one is completed. Completion is 0.5. After losing that plan, the surviving completion does not reveal whether the original denominator was 1, 2 or 4. Regenerating a plan cannot recover the historical answer.

**Why it matters:** Decision 57’s “losing a phone never lowers … the growth number” is not delivered. It also contradicts the claim that GrowthEngine reads only P entries.

**Concrete fix:** Specify immutable, synced daily scoring inputs: occurrence identities, scoring difficulties, denominator, rest status and effective revision. Establish their permitted classification explicitly. Recovery must use those retained inputs, never regenerated plans. Include days with zero completions in recovery tests.

**3. Critical — Generation isolation still has a zone-creation race.**  
**Files:** 16 §§B.8–B.9; 22 §B.4. **Continuation: R1 #3.**

**Problem:** The preflight check moved from record upload to zone creation:

1. B fetches Meta and confirms G is current.
2. B prepares a create/recreate operation for G.
3. A retires G and deletes its zones.
4. B’s delayed zone-creation operation executes.

The text asserts retired zones are never recreated, but the separate Meta fetch does not enforce that invariant. Concurrent deletions also lack a rule for whether a retry may replace an already-new current generation, leaving another generation orphaned.

**Why it matters:** Old personal data may persist again in CloudKit even if current-generation filtering keeps it out of the UI.

**Concrete fix:** Specify who may create generations and eliminate ordinary stale-client recreation of previously established generations. Define delayed creation, concurrent deletion, lost acknowledgement and cleanup behavior explicitly. Add these interleavings to the real-device tests.

**Platform uncertainty:** Apple documents zone creation/deletion and atomic record writes within custom zones; I did not establish a server-side conditional zone-creation operation tied to a Meta record. Do not assume that atomicity. [Apple CKRecordZone documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/cloudkit/ckrecordzone)

**4. Important — Cross-store recovery is incomplete outside the happy path.**  
**Files:** 17 §§B.8–B.9; 16 §B.9. **Continuation: R1 #12; also affects plan commits.**

**Problem:** In phone-only mode, an H2 Body revision and its P `pathRevised` entry occupy separate databases, yet §B.8 promises one transaction without a protocol. Import has a journal, but does not prevent projection, sync or deletion between its P and H2 commits. The journal is not explicitly bound to an account and generation.

**Why it matters:** A crash can expose half-applied changes. A resumed import after deletion could restore data the user just deleted.

**Concrete fix:** Use a defined recoverable command protocol for both plan creation and import. Bind journals to account/generation; specify read/upload visibility until completion; make deletion cancel and remove matching journals and staging files. Test crash → deletion → relaunch and crash → account switch → relaunch.

**5. Important — Correction semantics still leave builders choices that affect results.**  
**File:** 17 §B.7. **Continuation: R1 #9.**

**Problem:** A target existing locally does not imply a later wall-clock `recordedAt`; clocks can move backwards. Local creation rules also do not validate imported graphs. “Winning correction at the end of the chain” does not specify whether voiding the newest amendment restores the previous amendment or the original target.

**Why it matters:** Different replay implementations can calculate different histories or accept cyclic imported graphs.

**Concrete fix:** Define an acyclic correction graph and validate it on every ingestion path. Specify recursively how voided corrections are removed before winner selection, allowed amendment fields, and whether earlier amendments revive. Supply branching undo/redo vectors, including clock rollback and missing targets.

**6. Important — Vitality’s stated golden expectations are mathematically inconsistent.**  
**File:** 04 §§B.3, B.7.

I evaluated the formula with full presence before the absence, no planned rest, and today included as written:

| Case | Formula result | Stated expectation |
|---|---:|---:|
| First day off, today | **0.790799** | 1.0 |
| Three days off | **0.656911 or 0.623439**, depending on tie choice | ≈0.66 |
| Fourteen days off | **0.167361 or 0.15**, after the floor | ≈0.17 |
| Thirty days off | **Exactly the same possibilities as fourteen days** | 0.15 |

**Problem:** Today is excluded from the free-day substitution, so the first absence immediately dims Vitality by about 21%. Equal-lowest days have no tie-break. Fourteen and thirty absent days produce identical 14-day input windows, so the stated different outputs cannot follow from this formula.

**Why it matters:** This violates Part A’s free-day promise and prevents deterministic golden tests.

**Concrete fix:** Define a grace-day mechanism that covers the current absence; specify initialization, tie-breaking and inactivity duration separately from the window if needed. Recalculate the expectations from the corrected formula. Test morning-before-first-action separately from completed-day snapshots.

**7. Important — Comeback behavior is not a complete state machine.**  
**File:** 04 §B.3.

**Problem:** Testing whether the “most recent completed Action” followed three absent days stops identifying the comeback once a second Action arrives. Alternatively, searching backward for C preserves it—but that algorithm is not stated. The condition “presence is 0 again” also ends a comeback at the beginning of the next day before the user acts.

`max(V_raw, relightCurve(n))` makes 0.60/0.80/1.00 **minimums**, not unconditional expected values. A comeback after only three missed days can already have `V_raw > 0.60`.

**Why it matters:** The comeback can disappear after another completion, reset overnight or produce contradictory tests.

**Concrete fix:** Define deterministic start, continuation, interruption and completion transitions using finalized days. Retain or derive C independently of the latest Action. Specify planned-rest treatment and one-time sensory-event acknowledgement. Give separate vectors for short and long absences.

**8. Important — Adding an Action can lower Depth.**  
**File:** 04 §B.2.

**Problem:** Repeat factors depend on chronological position, so a late-arriving Action can displace a harder Action into a lower-paying position.

With no prior consistency history, two same-pillar Actions at `d=2` each receive the edge bonus:

`Depth = 2×1.2 + 2×1.2 = 4.8`.

Add an earlier, ordinary `d=0.5` Action:

`Depth = 0.5 + 2×1.2 + 2×0.5×1.2 = 4.1`.

**Why it matters:** A genuine additional Action reduces Depth by 0.7 without a correction or formula change, violating Part A and decision 51.

**Concrete fix:** Use an aggregation whose value cannot decrease when a positive Action is added—for example, a concave function of accumulated weighted effort. If retaining ranked contributions, prove the chosen ranking is monotonic under insertion. Add the example above as a mandatory vector.

**9. Important — Legacy offsets lack migration identity and correction attribution.**  
**Files:** 04 §B.2; 16 §B.3; 17 §B.3. **Continuation: R1 #8, with new formula-specific defects.**

**Problem:**

- Two offline devices can each append the same migration offset; no semantic de-duplication key exists.
- `Depth_old` is undefined as computed or displayed Depth. If displayed Depth includes previous offsets, calculating a new difference and then accumulating it can count old protection twice.
- Constant offsets retain mistaken growth: if one mistaken Action produced 100 under old rules and 50 under new rules, the offset is 50. Voiding that sole Action leaves displayed Depth 50.

**Why it matters:** Formula updates can inflate Depth, and removing a mistaken Action need not remove its protected contribution as Part A promises.

**Concrete fix:** Define a migration key, predecessor rules version, exact historical input boundary, one cumulative-offset equation, and deterministic merge behavior. Attribute protected contributions sufficiently to remove the portion belonging to corrected mistakes. Test two offline migrations, successive rule changes and voiding all historical Actions.

**10. Important — Day completion is neither historically fixed nor protected against non-correction decreases.**  
**File:** 04 §B.4; 17 §§B.3–B.4, B.8.

**Problem:** There is no rule fixing when `S(D)` becomes authoritative. Adding a scheduled item or syncing another plan revision can change a previously full day to a partial day, lowering G without a correction. Concurrent Spark swaps also leave more than one replacement without a scoring-set selection rule.

The fill rule contradicts its explanation: with scheduled difficulty total 2, four non-S Actions at difficulty 1 yield `fill=2`, allowing completion 1 with **none** of S completed. There is no separate “fill cap” despite the required vector naming one.

Formula changes can also lower historical G; the legacy protection covers only Depth.

**Why it matters:** The system can retract earned growth or reward behavior different from the approved description.

**Concrete fix:** Freeze/version daily scoring sets and their difficulties; define effective dates for revisions and deterministic swap reconciliation. Specify whether full substitution is allowed and encode the intended cap. Define how historical G survives rule changes without non-correction losses.

**11. Important — Level adaptation is not yet deterministic or fully represented in the record.**  
**Files:** 04 §§B.1, B.5; 17 §B.3.

**Problem:** Missing rules include:

- Whether the three-completion counter resets, or every subsequent completion triggers another increase.
- Whether “too easy” and the third completion apply one or two increases.
- Precedence when feedback and skip/completion triggers overlap.
- Definitions and stored events for skips, on-time completion and feedback.
- Which level determines `d`: scheduling-time or completion-time, before or after adaptation.
- A strictly positive initial level and handling of zero/missing HealthKit history.
- How a weekly-volume track maps to an individual session’s target.
- A deterministic method for “~1 in 5” feedback prompts.

`levelUpdated` is mentioned as working data but has no defined schema or replay/merge policy.

**Why it matters:** Implementations can diverge in difficulty, scoring and future recommendations from the same user history.

**Concrete fix:** Specify an ordered transition table with counters, resets, cooldowns and precedence; define immutable input events and level snapshots. Require compatible target/level units, positive denominators and finite values. Seed prompt selection deterministically or record its selection.

**12. Minor — The golden-vector list is mostly a test outline, not a reproducible contract.**  
**Files:** 04 §B.7; 16 §B.3.

Some expectations are correct:

- Consistency reaches **1.5 on day 28** with daily prior activity.
- The six repeat factors match the formula.
- `1.01^365 = 37.783434…`, so **37.78 is correct**.

But “voiding an Action lowers G” is not universally true: removing surplus effort can leave completion capped at 1. The multi-pillar and edge examples lack complete inputs and expected outputs.

**Why it matters:** Tests could validate different interpretations while all claiming compliance.

**Concrete fix:** Supply complete fixtures: history, scoring sets, `asOf`, settings, versions and expected values. Define numeric precision, rounding and tolerances. Include both capped and uncapped corrections. Specify whether same-day Actions occurring after `asOf` are excluded.

**C. Phase B verdict: no**

The module structure, immutable hashing, export matrix, Relay privacy classification and testing-environment changes are usable foundations. Approval still requires a consistent health-storage schema, recoverable scoring inputs, a deletion protocol covering zone creation, and complete correction/transaction semantics. These are architectural choices that implementation plans should not invent.

**D. Chapter 04 Part B verdict: no**

The growth ceiling calculation is correct, and equal treatment of verification is explicit. However, Vitality fails its own expectations, Depth can fall when an Action is added, and migration, daily-set and adaptation rules are not deterministic enough to implement. Correct those rules and supply executable, independently checked golden vectors before approving this chapter.
