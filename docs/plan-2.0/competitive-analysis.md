# Competitive analysis: Finch, Habitica, Fabulous (2026-10-07)

Why people pay for self-improvement apps, and what brings them back. Figures for private companies are estimates.

## At a glance

| | **Finch** | **Habitica** | **Fabulous** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What | Raise a little bird by taking care of yourself | Your life as a pixel RPG: tasks are quests | A science-based coach for habits and routines |
| Since | 2021 (Stephanie Yuan, Thomas Budi, ex-Quora) | 2013 (Kickstarter "HabitRPG") | 2014, Paris (Duke behavioral-economics roots) |
| Money | ~$30–48M a year, bootstrapped, very profitable | ~$5M a year | Once large; now ~$1M a year by app-tracker estimates |
| Price | ~$70/yr (often less), monthly ~$10 | $48/yr or $5/mo + gems | ~$40–50/yr, 7-day trial |
| Free tier | Very generous, no ads | The whole core game is free | Thin: the first journey, then the wall |
| Retention | Best-in-class (D30 above Duolingo, Calm, Headspace) | Loyal hardcore, steep drop-off for others | Strong start, churn and billing complaints |

## Finch: the winner

**Why people pay:** not to unlock the core. The core is free, and the bird and caring for yourself never sit behind the paywall. People pay for *more of what they love*:
- more outfits and furniture for the bird;
- more soundscapes and breathing sessions;
- and, above all, to support something that helped them.

The paywall shows after the onboarding, with a free trial and discounts.

**What brings them back:**
1. **A creature that depends on you:** you raise it from egg to hatchling to adult. Guilt-free, but you *want* to check in.
2. **Daily goals as energy:** each tiny goal tapped gives the bird energy. The reward is the tap itself.
3. **Adventures:** when energy is full, the bird leaves on an adventure for about 8 real hours and comes back with a story. That's two or more visits a day, without nagging.
4. **Rainbow Stones:** a currency earned by doing, spent on outfits and decor. Never pay-to-win.
5. **A streak, added in 2025,** gentle and forgiving.
6. **Friends ("Tree Town"):** send small "good vibes" to friends' birds. Warm, never competitive.
7. **A starter plan from the onboarding,** so there's never an empty first day.

**Weakness:** it's light on real outcomes, closer to an emotional support app than a growth program.

## Habitica: the closest mechanical cousin

**Why people pay:** cosmetics, and supporting an indie, open-source project. Paying never gives an advantage ("play to win, not pay to win"):
- monthly gems and mystery gear;
- buying gems with gold.

That's why its revenue stays modest.

**What brings them back:**
1. **Consequences:** missed daily tasks cost you health. If you "die", you lose a level, gold and an item. Real stakes, and it motivates some people strongly.
2. **Party accountability:** in a boss quest, *your* missed dailies hurt *the whole party*. Friends depend on you. This is its strongest hook.
3. **Loot:** gold, XP and random drops (eggs, hatching potions, pets, then mounts). A variable reward on every completion.
4. **Custom rewards:** you set your own rewards ("watch an episode = 20 gold"), so real life becomes the shop.
5. **Guilds and challenges** with gem prizes.

**Weakness:** a dated interface. Setup is all on you, with no plan, no coach and no guidance. The punishment burns people out, and many quit after a bad week.

## Fabulous: the cautionary tale

**Why people paid:** a credible promise ("science-based, from Duke"), beautiful design, and a personal program. Its journeys walk you through Foundations → Struggle → Mastery, with morning, afternoon and evening routines, coaching audio and letters.

**What brings them back:**
1. **A structured journey** that unlocks step by step. You want to see the next letter or chapter.
2. **Rituals tied to times of day,** with reminders at each one.
3. **Habit stacking:** one tiny habit (drink water), then add the next.
4. **A commitment contract** you "sign" at the start.

**Why it shrank:**
- an aggressive trial-to-annual paywall and confusing prices;
- notifications that are hard to turn off;
- data asked for before trust is earned;
- a growth trick disguised as a gratitude exercise.

Users felt pushed rather than helped. Revenue estimates fell from a large peak to about $1M a year.

## Why people pay, in one line each
- **Finch:** "I love this little bird and it helps me. I'll give back." Emotional connection, and a generous free tier.
- **Habitica:** "I want the cute gear, and to support the devs." Cosmetics and community.
- **Fabulous:** "This will fix my life, scientifically." A promise of results, and conversion at first sight.

The lasting model is Finch's: give the core away, make people *love* it, and sell *more of the joy*, never the right to make progress.

## What keeps people coming back: the patterns
1. **Something that waits for you** (Finch's bird, Hush's Spark): care rather than guilt.
2. **Reasons to come back more than once a day** (Finch's adventures).
3. **Tiny daily wins with an instant reward** (all three).
4. **A currency earned only by doing,** spent on cosmetics (Finch's stones, Habitica's gold).
5. **People who count on you** (Habitica's party is the strongest). Friends, not leaderboards.
6. **A path with chapters** (Fabulous's journeys, Hush's worlds).
7. **Forgiveness:** streaks that bend rather than break. Habitica's punishment drives many people away.

## For Hush
Already aligned:
- the Spark (Finch's bird);
- Embers earned only by living;
- the protected streak with shields and repair;
- friends who cheer;
- worlds as chapters;
- a starter plan from the intro;
- no paywall during onboarding.

Worth adding (proposals):
1. **A second daily visit:** the Spark goes on a short journey into a world after your quests and comes back hours later with a story, a Book page and a few Embers (Finch's adventure, in our lore).
2. **Companions in a small party:** opt-in, 2–4 friends lighting the same realm together. Your day helps theirs, and a missed day never *hurts* them; it just doesn't help (Habitica's accountability, without the punishment).
3. **Real-life rewards in the shop:** you set your own treats, bought with Embers (Habitica).
4. **Variable surprises:** a rare find on some quests (a Book page, a cosmetic, a Keeper's line).
5. **Monetization (later):** the core stays free forever. The subscription sells *more joy*: more cosmetics for the Spark and worlds, more journeys and sessions, the Companion's deeper plans. Never progress, worlds or streak protection for money (decision 81).
6. **Avoid Fabulous's mistakes:** clear pricing, a quiet reminder you control, trust before data.
